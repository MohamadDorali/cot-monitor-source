User guide

What this tool shows

Weekly positioning of futures traders from the CFTC Commitments of Traders (COT) reports, and where today's positioning sits compared with the last 2, 3 and 5 years. Positions are as of Tuesday and published on Friday afternoon (New York time); the page updates automatically after each release.

Data: Futures Only or Futures + Options

The Data switch at the top of the page, above the filters, switches between Futures Only and Futures + Options (the CFTC combined reports, which add options positions converted to futures equivalents). Every table, percentile and chart follows the selected data set. Open interest differs between the two, so values and percentiles differ slightly.

Finding your way around

Markets switch (top of the page): Featured shows the main markets, All shows every active contract, and My list shows the markets you starred (☆). Starred markets are remembered in your browser. Searching always looks across all markets.

Quick view buttons: Extremes keeps markets where a percentile is at or above 90 or at or below 10; Biggest weekly moves lists the 30 markets whose net position changed most this week, measured as a share of open interest.

Trend column: a small line of the net position over the last 5 years, scaled to its own range; the dot is the latest value. It is the same view as the percentile, just visual.

Sharing: the page address always contains the current view (data set, filters, market, chart settings), so you can copy it and send it, and the Back button works as expected.

Markets

All active contracts in the CFTC reports are included (choose All in the Markets switch) and grouped (currencies, crypto, equity indices, rates, metals, energy, natural gas, power and environmental, grains, softs, livestock, dairy, other). New contracts appear automatically. Use the search box (names and tickers such as GC, CL, ES) and the group buttons to narrow the table, click a column heading to sort within each group, and click a group name to collapse or expand it (Collapse all and Expand all are above the table).

Some markets are young or thinly traded and have less than 5 years of data. For these, the percentile uses all the history available inside the window and its percentile is based on fewer weeks than the window; hover over the percentile to see how many. A percentile is shown once there are at least 26 weeks of data. Charts simply start where the data starts.

Reports and trader categories

Legacy covers every market. Non-Commercial are large speculators (hedge funds and similar), Commercial are hedgers using futures against a physical or financial business, and Non-Reportable are small traders below the reporting threshold.

Disaggregated covers commodities (metals, energy, agriculture): Managed Money (funds and CTAs), Producer/Merchant, Swap Dealers, Other Reportables, Non-Reportable.

TFF (Traders in Financial Futures) covers currencies, equity indices, rates and crypto: Leveraged Funds (hedge funds), Asset Manager (institutional), Dealer/Intermediary, Other Reportables, Non-Reportable.

Table columns

  • Long / Short: contracts held long or short by the selected category.
  • Total: Long + Short.
  • % Long / % Short: each side as a share of Total. Blue means mostly long, red mostly short.
  • Net: Long − Short. Positive is net long, negative is net short.
  • Net % OI: Net divided by total open interest. Because market size grows over time, this makes today comparable with years ago.
  • Δ Net (week) and % Chg: change in net position since the previous report, in contracts and as a percent of last week's absolute net.
  • Pctl 2Y / 3Y / 5Y: percentile rank of the current value inside the last 2, 3 or 5 years. 100 is the most net long of the window, 0 the most net short, 50 the middle. Colors run from red (low) through white to blue (high).

The Percentile of selector switches the percentiles between raw net position and net as % of open interest.

Charts

Click a row, or open the Charts tab (use Find to locate a market quickly). Use ← Table to go back and the arrows next to the market name to step through the markets in the same order as the table. For a single category and Net, Long or Short, the chart shows the line over the chosen window with: the blue zone above the 90th percentile and the red zone below the 10th percentile (the extreme zones), dotted max and min lines, a grey median line, and a marker for the latest value with its percentile. Choose "All three" to see the 2, 3 and 5 year windows stacked; the default is 5 years.

Long and short together, or all categories together, are drawn as plain lines without zones.

Exporting

Table: the Export menu offers CSV (raw numbers), Excel (.xlsx with the same colors as the table) and a PNG image of the table that is ready to share. All three contain exactly what is shown (selected data set, report, category and filters). Charts: each chart has an Export menu with PNG, JPEG, WebP and SVG images (with title and source line added) and a CSV of the plotted data for that window.

On a phone or tablet

Markets are shown as cards (percentiles at the top, then net position and weekly change); use Sort to reorder them and Filters to change report, category and basis. Touch and hold a label or button to see its help text.

How to read it

A high percentile means the category is unusually long compared with the window; a low one means unusually short. Extreme readings show crowded positioning, which is context for risk and sentiment rather than a timing signal on its own. When comparing across long periods, prefer the % of open interest view.

Percentile definition: the share of weeks in the window, including the current week, whose value is at or below the current value.
